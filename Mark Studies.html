Three takes on the mark

The current mark reads clean but spare — a triangle with a single dot. These three directions keep that geometry and the muted-teal accent, but each gives the eye more to say: one makes it literal, one makes it deep, one makes it radiant.

01 · Lens

An eye you can actually read

The dot opens into an almond lens with a pupil — the most direct reading of "eye." It still nests inside the same triangle, so the silhouette is unchanged at a glance, but up close it gains a humanist, watchful character. Best when the mark sits large: covers, the landing masthead, an app icon.

02 · Aperture

Looking deeper into the filing

Concentric triangles draw the eye inward to the pupil — a lens stopping down, focus tightening. It's the most conceptual fit for the product: reading past the surface of a disclosure to the thing that actually changed. The nested strokes also give the small mark more texture than a lone dot.

03 · Radiant

The seal, in our voice

An iris ring plus a short burst of rays above the apex — the most overt nod to the dollar-bill emblem, pulled back to a thin, restrained engraving rather than anything ornate. It carries authority and a quiet wink. Reads beautifully at hero scale; the rays simplify gracefully toward favicon size.
